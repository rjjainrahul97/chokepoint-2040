# 2D cartoon asset request and character integration plan

Build: CP2040-20261114-DEBT-BRIEF. Asset status: REQUESTED, NOT SUPPLIED. Character mechanics status: PLANNED, NOT SHIPPED. All representatives are fictional; their agendas are game assumptions, not statements about real leaders or countries.

## Copy this brief to the content creator

Create original **2D cartoon portraits** for a lightweight mobile geopolitical strategy game. Use clean vector-like outlines, flat colours, a little two-tone cel shading, expressive but restrained faces and a consistent editorial-cartoon style. No photorealism, 3D rendering, anime exaggeration, real politician/CEO likenesses, national caricatures or borrowed game artwork.

For every character: one separate **512 x 512 PNG with a transparent background**, head and shoulders, facing three-quarter towards the viewer, both eyes visible, gaze near camera, shoulders inside the canvas, about 10% clear margin, neutral attentive expression. Match head scale, camera distance, line weight and lighting across the entire cast. The game background is dark teal (#07151c), with warm gold (#edbe7b), pale text and muted blue-green accents. Keep clothing silhouettes readable at 96px.

No words, labels, flags, logos, scenery, frame, speech bubble or watermark within the image. UI supplies the name, country/company and dialogue. Deliver individual files with the exact names below, plus an optional contact sheet. Do not substitute a contact sheet for the individual transparent files. Keep the two representatives of each institution visibly distinct. Their appearance must not imply that one is a villain and one a hero.

Start with **batch 1: eight portraits** (two each for India, China, Germany and Meridian Machines). Then make batch 2: the other thirty. No backgrounds, cinematics or full-body poses are needed. One neutral portrait per person is enough for the first release. Optional later expression variants are pleased/concerned/firm, preserving the exact person's design; do not make them now unless requested.

Per-image prompt: “Create one original flat 2D cartoon head-and-shoulders portrait in the shared CHOKEPOINT art style above. Subject: [name], a fictional [role]. Appearance: [appearance]. Neutral attentive expression. Transparent 512x512 PNG. No text, logo, flag or scenery. Keep the face readable at small mobile size. Filename: [filename].”

## How images will be used

1. On the relevant event, show one active representative beside the offer, warning or counteroffer, normally at 72-112 CSS pixels. Use the same person in that institution's pending negotiation and consequence report. Show the player's own envoy only for cabinet/leadership moments. Do not place nineteen portraits in the main dashboard.
2. Relation tone controls a border, short label and dialogue, not a random different face. The neutral asset covers ordinary, friendly and tense encounters. Later expression variants may enrich it without being essential.
3. Leaders change only through a resolved leadership review or company succession event. The incoming character's asset and name are used from that period onward. Historical entries retain the old speaker. Every line records speaker ID, event ID and the actual state reason.
4. Original PNGs are source assets. On import, make compressed 192px WebP runtime derivatives and a small fallback, keeping original sources in a separate path. Initial request aims for 8-18KB per runtime portrait, subject to actual inspection and compression. Load only the current encounter and optionally preload one likely next speaker. Set fixed image dimensions, lazy loading and asynchronous decoding. No runtime image generation, remote image service or large preload grid.
5. If an image is missing or fails, render the country flag/company initial plus the speaker name. A missing portrait must never block a decision or break a save.
6. Keep manifest status honest. It currently describes requested assets, not existing image files. Once supplied, record source Library file ID, licence/provenance, target filename, checksum, dimensions, derivative size and integration verification. No URL should pretend that a portrait already exists.

## Leadership, political shifts and corporate succession

The requested 2027-2035 campaign is **nine years**, so it does not contain more than two complete five-year terms. Preserve that horizon. Two alternative representatives can support several successive administrations, including a returning incumbent; a unique portrait for every period is unnecessary.

[ASM] For the 2035 ruleset, consider scenario leadership reviews at 2031 H1 and 2035 H1, with country-specific review mechanisms. These are fictional gameplay review windows, not claims about real constitutional election calendars. In a retained 2040 legacy campaign, five-year reviews could instead occur in 2032 and 2037. Do not extend the game or impose elections on every regime just to fit the art.

- A leadership review evaluates living standards, approval, essential-service delivery and public debt. Staying in office retains the current representative; a mandate shift can replace A with B or eventually return A. Review choices should concern funding priorities or renegotiation, not a free portrait selection with arbitrary bonuses.
- The new representative changes policy utility and bargaining priorities. The country keeps its assets, outstanding loans, enforceable contracts and institutional relationships. A change of leader does not cancel debt or erase sanctions.
- Separate institutional trust from a bounded personal negotiating disposition. Do not reset relations to 50 when a new face arrives. Repeated breaches still matter to the successor.
- Companies have board/succession reviews when persistent poor returns, regulation or a strategic acquisition makes change plausible. They do not all replace CEOs every election. Assets, IP and contractual liabilities persist through succession; the new executive changes what they are willing to negotiate.
- Resolve at most one leadership/succession development in the foreground per ordinary turn. Other actor changes can wait in the inbox or a compact report. Do not turn the game into nineteen personnel decisions.

## Dialogue and scenario examples

These lines are authored examples, not an implemented branching system. Actual offers must satisfy the engine's eligibility and counterparty utility checks.

| Speaker/event | Line | Required mechanical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Mara Keller / Germany has a proven process and needs manufacturing scale | “We have the process. You have the market. Fund the pilot and we can license production, but the patent stays with us.” | Quote a real licence, upfront cost, royalty, rights and time. India must have the required complementary inputs. |
| Kabir Sethi / incoming Indian mandate with stressed households | “The factories stay. But the next budget must show what households get for the debt.” | Change available priority packages and negotiation utility; do not delete existing factories or loans. |
| Lin Yue / competitor capacity threatens margins | “We can beat that input price if you commit to three years of purchases.” | Offer a viable discounted offtake with a minimum purchase obligation and disclosed exit cost. |
| Chen Wei / cooperation offers more value than restriction | “We both lose from a supply freeze. Let us trade access for a dependable order book.” | Conditional continued supply or reciprocal access; no automatic goodwill dividend. |
| Emi Takeda / licensed process lacks sublicensing rights | “You may manufacture with our equipment. Selling the design to someone else is a different agreement.” | Block unauthorised sublicensing and offer a priced rights extension if the company is willing. |
| Luca Marin / successor pursues volume | “Guarantee the orders and we can train your process engineers.” | Offer lower licensing friction in exchange for real volume commitments and training delay. |
| Morgan Vale / strong lock-in | “Your workloads already run on our stack. A renewal avoids the migration bill.” | Disclose actual switching costs and a contract renewal; alternative migration remains possible at its cost. |
| Priya Sen / regulation makes lock-in less profitable | “Open interfaces could keep your business with us. Here is what that costs.” | Counteroffer interoperability with real costs/access changes, rather than unconditional capitulation. |

Leverage comes from controlled assets and agreements, not a threatening expression. Both representatives can cooperate or apply pressure when their incentives support it. Dialogue must state the actual consequence and cannot introduce an unsupported block.
